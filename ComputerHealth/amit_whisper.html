What this is

Amit Live is how Amit hears you. Speak, and Amit types your words into any program on your computer: a message box, a search box, your code editor — anywhere you can type.

How to use it

  1. Click inside a text box so it has the cursor. Amit types into the box that has focus. If no text box is active, the Dictate window opens so you can keep talking.
  2. Hold the right mouse button until the Amit Live ring opens around your pointer. Let go, then click Dictate. (Press Esc to close the ring without choosing.)
  3. Speak. Your words appear as you talk, and Amit tidies them up when you finish a sentence. Press the button once to stop. (If “Press Enter when I let go” is on below, Amit also presses Enter to send when you stop.)

The Amit Helper bar, on the right edge of your screen, opens with a double right-click.

While you speak, your voice goes to your browser’s speech service — the same one Amit’s chat uses. Amit does not record or keep your voice. You can leave this window minimized, or close it: Amit Live starts it again when you next need it.

Keep Amit Live ready:
While Amit Live is ready, it keeps a small window running in the background so it can start listening at once. That window uses about 400 MB of your computer’s memory (RAM) — up to about half a gigabyte — for as long as it stays on. If you keep it running all day, that memory is in use all day. Turning it off frees the memory; the next time you use it, it takes a few seconds to get ready.

Amit Hub
