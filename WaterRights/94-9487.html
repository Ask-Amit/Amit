Statutory Research — Idaho Code Title 42, Chapter 2

§ 42-204 — Extensions of Time

Initial development period: 5 years from permit approval to complete construction and prove beneficial use.

Paragraph (a): Delays caused by federal/state/local government approval processes or litigation — time lost is added back to the deadline.

Paragraph (d): Permits authorizing 2+ cfs or 100+ acres can get an additional extension of up to 10 years. (94-9487's 1.25 cfs is under this threshold, so this route wasn't available.)

Paragraph (f): General catch-all — one extension of time, not exceeding 5 years, for good cause. This is the one Glen used in 2021. Subsection (6): the Department will not grant a second extension under this paragraph once one has been granted.

§ 42-218 — Proof of Beneficial Use

Total time (original period + all extensions) cannot exceed the maximum prescribed in § 42-204. Under paragraph (f): 5 years original + 5 years for the one extension = 10 years total — which is exactly where permit 94-9487 sits as of September 1, 2026.

§ 42-218a — Lapse and Reinstatement

If proof of beneficial use or an extension request isn't filed by the deadline, the permit lapses. Reinstatement is possible within 60 days on a showing of "reasonable cause," or later with a certified water examiner's report, a written statement of reasonable cause, and a $250 fee — with the priority date moved forward to the date proof is actually filed.

§ 42-223 — Exceptions/Defenses to Forfeiture

Applies to already-licensed rights, not developing permits. Includes a "circumstances beyond the owner's control" exception — potentially relevant if this permit reaches license stage and later faces a non-use question.

What happens after filing

  • The form goes to the state office, then gets scheduled for a field examiner visit — likely sometime in the next year.
  • The examiner inspects both wells, verifies industrial, fire protection, and commercial use, takes photos, and runs flow tests.
  • The final license is sized to actual verified use — this could be less than the 1.25 cfs currently on the permit.
  • Water use is fully legal right now, during the permit period — the license (once issued) is what makes the right permanent and attached to the property long-term.
