conventions.md

Why F and J

This convention came out of live testing against Ryan's actual microphone, not guesswork. A few things were tried and ruled out on the spot:

Single unstressed words don't get misheard — they disappear. A word like "Selah" lost its trailing sound entirely on repeat tries. A candidate needs a hard landing sound or it vanishes rather than transcribing wrong — and a whole extra word isn't needed anyway.
Two-letter combos spoken together are unreliable. Saying "FJ" repeatedly produced completely different results each time — "Jitter," "F enter," "F tab" — never the same thing twice. Not usable as a trigger.
Single letters, said in isolation or embedded in a full sentence, held up. F, J, X, Q, Z, and H all transcribed cleanly and consistently once tested properly.

The working convention

F / J — the magic words themselves. No separate activation phrase — say or type F or J directly, followed by the command, and Amit treats it as a shortcut instruction rather than normal conversation. Right and left index fingers, home-row keys, for anyone typing instead of speaking.

What a shortcut actually does right now

A simple shortcut is a stored instruction — when triggered, Amit treats it as if you'd typed that instruction directly into the conversation.

A master shortcut bundles subtasks — triggering it runs each subtask's instruction in sequence, in one command.

Open question, not yet resolved: whether a shortcut should be able to fire automatically inside a live Claude Code session (would need a hook watching for the trigger text) versus staying a reference panel you read before typing the instruction yourself. See AmitCoder/CLAUDE.md.
