How This Was Actually Built

Not a highlight reel. The real exchanges from the actual session that built the Medical Prep tool — what Ryan asked for, in his own words, and what Amit built or fixed in response. Including the parts that went wrong the first time and had to be caught and corrected.

Hey — this page exists because Ryan wanted you to actually see how this works, instead of just being told about it. He knows what it looks like from the outside: someone talking to a computer for hours. He gets why that reads strange, maybe even a little concerning, if you don't know what's actually happening underneath it.

So here's what's actually happening, plainly. Ryan doesn't type a wish and get a finished app. He directs — sometimes precisely, sometimes in a rough voice-to-text sentence with a typo or two — and Amit builds a real, working attempt. Then Ryan tests it himself, or hands it to someone else to test (that's you, a few messages down in this timeline), and reports back exactly what's wrong. Amit doesn't get defensive about it or pretend it worked. It goes and finds the actual cause and fixes it — sometimes admitting a real mistake, like guessing at a Hebrew letter that turned out not to mean anything, or building something that silently broke because it was too big to paste into a chat box. That back-and-forth, dozens of times, over one afternoon, is what actually built the tool you're looking at.

That's not magic and it's not make-believe. It's the same thing two developers do when they pair up — one person directs and tests, the other builds and reports back — except one of the two happens to be an AI that can write and fix code fast, in Ryan's own words, following his own real corrections. Below is the actual record. Real quotes on the left, exactly what got built on the right. Read as much or as little as you want.
