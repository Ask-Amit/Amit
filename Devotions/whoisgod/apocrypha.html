Additional Texts — Evaluation & Inclusion Criteria

Criteria for inclusion: (1) Quoted or referenced in the canonical NT. (2) Found among the Dead Sea Scrolls, indicating pre-Christian Jewish usage. (3) Included in the Septuagint (LXX), the Bible the apostles used. (4) Consistent with canonical theology — does not contradict Torah, Prophets, or NT. (5) Provides historical witness to practices or doctrines relevant to the covenant question.

Categories: INCLUDE = meets 3+ criteria, strong case for canonical-adjacent authority. REFERENCE = meets 2 criteria, valuable for context but not doctrinal authority. EXCLUDE = fails criteria, contradicts canon, or late composition with no apostolic attestation.

1 Enoch (Ethiopic Enoch) INCLUDE

Jude 14-15 directly quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 | 2 Peter 2:4 references Enoch's Watcher narrative | Found in Dead Sea Scrolls (4Q201-212) | Canonical in Ethiopian Orthodox Church
✓ Directly quoted in canonical NT (Jude 14-15)
✓ Multiple copies found at Qumran (DSS) — pre-Christian usage confirmed
✓ Referenced by early church fathers (Barnabas, Clement, Tertullian, Origen)
✓ Provides theology of fallen angels, origin of evil, Messianic prophecy consistent with canonical themes
△ Sections 37-71 (Parables of Enoch) not found at Qumran — possibly later composition

Relevance to covenant question: 1 Enoch 99:2 — "Woe to those who alter the words of truth and change the eternal covenant." Enoch 5:4 describes the righteous as those who "do not transgress the eternal commandments." 1 Enoch attributes the teaching of forbidden practices (sorcery, astrology, cosmetics for seduction) to the fallen Watchers — suggesting that corruption of God's original instructions has a supernatural origin, not merely a human one.

Jubilees (Little Genesis) INCLUDE

Found extensively at Qumran (15+ manuscripts — more copies than some canonical books) | Canonical in Ethiopian Orthodox Church | Referenced in Damascus Document (CD 16:3-4)
✓ 15+ copies at Qumran — one of the most popular texts in the DSS community
✓ Provides detailed feast and Sabbath observance predating Sinai (Abraham, Noah, and Jacob keep feasts)
✓ Solar calendar system consistent with Genesis 1 (lights for "signs and seasons")
✓ Theological consistency with canonical Torah on covenant, commandments, and judgment
△ Not quoted directly in the NT (referenced indirectly at most)

Relevance to covenant question: Jubilees is critical because it records the patriarchs keeping Sabbath (Jub 2:17-33), Shavuot (Jub 6:17-22), and Sukkot (Jub 16:20-31) before Sinai. This corroborates Genesis 26:5 (Abraham kept Torah) and demolishes the argument that these observances were temporary Sinai-era inventions. If the patriarchs kept them pre-Sinai and the prophets say they'll be kept post-Messiah's return, the "Sinai parenthesis" theory collapses.

1 Maccabees INCLUDE

In Septuagint (LXX) | Canonical in Catholic and Orthodox traditions | Historical account verified by Josephus | Hanukkah referenced in John 10:22
✓ In the LXX — the Bible the apostles used
✓ Historically verified — reliable account of Maccabean revolt
✓ Yeshua was at the Temple for Hanukkah (John 10:22) — the feast 1 Maccabees records
✓ Documents faithful Jews dying rather than violate Torah (dietary laws, Sabbath, circumcision)

Relevance to covenant question: 1 Maccabees records the Hellenization crisis — when a foreign empire tried to abolish Torah observance, forbid Sabbath, destroy copies of Torah, and force consumption of unclean food. Faithful Jews died rather than comply. This is the historical precedent for understanding Revelation's end-times scenario: a beast power that attempts to "change times and law" (Daniel 7:25). The Maccabean martyrs considered Torah observance worth dying for — not a temporary arrangement.

2 Maccabees REFERENCE

In Septuagint (LXX) | Canonical in Catholic/Orthodox | Contains earliest reference to creation ex nihilo (7:28) and resurrection of the body (7:9-14)
✓ In the LXX
✓ Theological contributions: creation ex nihilo, bodily resurrection, intercessory prayer
△ Includes prayer for the dead (12:43-45) — basis for Catholic purgatory doctrine, contested
△ More theological than historical — less reliable than 1 Maccabees

Relevance: 2 Maccabees 7 records the mother and seven sons who chose death over eating pork — demonstrating that second-temple Judaism universally understood dietary laws as non-negotiable. Their theology of resurrection (7:9, 14, 23) aligns with Daniel 12:2 and NT resurrection teaching.

Wisdom of Solomon REFERENCE

In the LXX | Quoted/echoed in Romans 1:18-32, Romans 9:21, Hebrews 1:3 | Early church fathers treated it as canonical
✓ Strong verbal parallels to Paul's writings (Romans 1 closely mirrors Wisdom 13-14)
✓ In the LXX
△ Likely composed in Greek, not translated from Hebrew — later composition (~100 BC–40 AD)
△ Some Platonic philosophical influences (immortality of the soul distinct from resurrection)

Relevance: Wisdom 2:12-20 contains a striking prophecy of the righteous sufferer that closely parallels the Passion narratives. Wisdom 6:18 ties love of wisdom to "keeping her laws" — reinforcing that Torah-observance and wisdom are inseparable.

Sirach (Ecclesiasticus) REFERENCE

In the LXX | Hebrew fragments found at Qumran and Masada | James may echo Sirach extensively | Canonical in Catholic/Orthodox
✓ Hebrew original confirmed by DSS/Masada finds
✓ In the LXX
✓ Strong thematic parallels to James (verbal echoes throughout)
△ Later composition (~180 BC) — post-prophetic era

Relevance: Sirach 24:23 identifies Wisdom with "the Torah that Moses commanded" — directly equating God's wisdom with Torah. This is consistent with Psalm 19:7 ("The Torah of YHWH is perfect") and Proverbs 8 (Wisdom present at creation). If Wisdom = Torah = eternal, then Torah-abolition = Wisdom-abolition.

Book of Jasher REFERENCE

Referenced in Joshua 10:13 and 2 Samuel 1:18 | Current versions may not be the original referenced text
✓ Explicitly named in canonical scripture as a source
△ The surviving "Book of Jasher" may not be the same text Joshua/Samuel reference
△ No DSS confirmation

Relevance: If the version available today is authentic (debated), it provides pre-Sinai patriarchal accounts consistent with Torah-keeping before Moses. Use with caution — the chain of custody is uncertain.

Relevance: 2 Esdras 7:24 — "They despised the Most High, and were contemptuous of His Torah, and abandoned His ways." Consistent with the prophetic pattern: covenant faithfulness = Torah-keeping; apostasy = Torah-abandonment.

Gospel of Thomas EXCLUDE

✗ Not in LXX or any canonical list
✗ Gnostic theological framework contradicts canonical theology
✗ Late composition (earliest manuscripts c. AD 200, the Oxyrhynchus papyri)
✗ No DSS presence

Book of Mormon / D&C / Pearl of Great Price EXCLUDE

✗ 19th century composition — no ancient manuscript evidence
✗ No DSS, LXX, or early church attestation
✗ Theological claims (God was once a man, eternal progression) contradict canonical monotheism
✗ Violates Deut 4:2 and Rev 22:18 — adds to scripture

Didache (Teaching of the Twelve Apostles) REFERENCE

Dated ~50-120 AD | Earliest known church manual | Nearly included in some early canons
✓ Very early composition — possibly contemporary with NT writings
✓ Describes early church practice: fasting on Wednesday/Friday (not Sunday), baptism formulas, eucharist prayers
✓ Theologically consistent with canonical NT
△ Not in final canon of any major tradition

Relevance: The Didache reveals early church practices that predate later institutional changes. It shows a community still operating within a Jewish framework while incorporating Gentile believers — consistent with Acts 15 and the Torah-continuity model.
