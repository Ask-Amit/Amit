The Seven Lampstands — Where Every Church Stands

How to read this map. "In the midst of the seven lampstands, one like a son of man" (Rev 1:12-13). He is the center and the standard. Around Him, on the outer shell, are the seven churches He wrote to, each shown as a lit lampstand. Every church on the scorecard is placed by two things. Its distance from the center shows how far it falls from the mark: the gold target is 170 of 170, the dashed rings mark 90%, 80%, 70% and 60% match, and everything beyond is below 60%. Its direction shows which letters it leans toward, taken from the percentages on the Seven Churches tab. Philadelphia sits at the top and Laodicea at the bottom, the faithful against the lukewarm. Ephesus and Thyatira pull to opposite sides, precision against love without testing. A dot's color is its strongest tendency. Drag to turn the sphere, scroll to zoom, and click any dot to see how far that church is from the mark, which letters it leans toward, and where it is weakest.
Click a dot, or choose a church or fellowship from the list, to see how close it is to the mark, which letters it leans toward, and where it is weakest. Click one of the seven candles to read that church's letter and how it lines up with Scripture.

What this picture can and cannot say

This is a picture of Amit's own numbers, not new evidence. Seven tendencies are folded into three dimensions, so closeness on the sphere is a guide, and the numbers in the side panel are the record. Each church has been assessed on the letters where it ranks in the top 20; the rest are drawn at a small baseline and are not shown as pulls. Distance from the mark is the scorecard total, which measures a movement's published doctrine and practice and not any person's walk. Tendencies are patterns, not verdicts, and most movements carry several at once. Only Yeshua stands at the center. Click "See the full score review" on any church to read why it scores where it does.
