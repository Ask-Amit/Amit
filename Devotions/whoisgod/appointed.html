"These Are My Appointed Times" — Why the Feasts Are Evidence

Lev 23:1-4 | Ex 12:14 | Lev 23:14, 21, 31, 41

Leviticus 23 opens with Yahweh's own possessive: "These are the appointed times of Yahweh… My appointed times" (Lev 23:2). The Hebrew word is מוֹעֲדִים (mo'adim): appointments, set meetings, fixed in advance by the one who keeps them. They are not called Israel's holidays. They are His calendar. And He attaches "a statute forever" to them again and again: Passover (Ex 12:14), Firstfruits (Lev 23:14), Shavuot (23:21), Yom Kippur (23:31), Sukkot (23:41).

That makes them testable in a way most theology is not. An appointment is a claim about when. If Yahweh says, centuries ahead of time, "this is the day," and the thing the day pictures then happens on that day, the calendar has done something no human author can arrange from the far side of history. The spring feasts give us four chances to check that. The fall feasts tell us what to expect next.

The Year at a Glance

The Hebrew year read clockwise from Nisan (spring) at the top. Gold marks the spring feasts, fulfilled at Messiah's first coming. Navy marks the fall feasts, still ahead. The dashed stretch between them is the long summer gap, and it is where we are living now. Positions are approximate; the spring cluster falls within about a week.

  1. Passover — 14 Nisan
  2. Unleavened Bread — 15–21 Nisan
  3. Firstfruits — during Unleavened Bread
  4. Shavuot — 50 days later (Sivan)
  5. Trumpets — 1 Tishrei
  6. Yom Kippur — 10 Tishrei
  7. Sukkot — 15–21 Tishrei

The Spring Feasts — The Model the Fall Feasts Follow

Ex 12 | 1 Cor 5:7-8 | Lev 23:10-11 | 1 Cor 15:20 | Ex 19:1 | Acts 2:1 | Jer 31:33

Every spring feast carries two fulfillments: a first one in Israel's own history, and a second one in Messiah. Both are real, and neither cancels the other. And the second fell on the feast's own day.

1. Passover — Pesach (14 Nisan)

First: the night in Egypt when the blood of a lamb on the doorposts caused the destroyer to pass over every house that trusted it (Exodus 12). Then: Yeshua, crucified at Passover. "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7). His blood is the covering that makes judgment pass over anyone who trusts in Him.

2. Unleavened Bread — Chag HaMatzot (15–21 Nisan)

First: Israel fled Egypt with no time for dough to rise, and leaven became scripture's own picture of sin working its way through something whole. Then: Yeshua, the sinless bread, unleavened, lay in the grave during this feast. Paul draws the line himself: "Cleanse out the old leaven… let us celebrate the festival… with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8).

3. Firstfruits — Bikkurim (during Unleavened Bread)

First: the first cutting of the barley harvest, waved before Yahweh as a pledge that the fuller harvest was still coming (Leviticus 23:10-11). Then: Yeshua rose from the dead on Firstfruits. "Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep" (1 Corinthians 15:20). He is the guarantee that everyone who belongs to Him will be raised the same way.

4. Shavuot — Pentecost (fifty days later)

First: the wheat harvest. Jewish tradition also ties this feast to the giving of Torah at Sinai. That link is tradition rather than an explicit statement in the text, but Exodus 19:1 does place Israel at Sinai in the third month, the month Shavuot falls in. Then: on that exact day, the Spirit was poured out in Acts 2. The same Torah once written on stone was now being written on the heart, exactly as Jeremiah 31:33 said it one day would be.

The Pattern: That is four feasts with four fulfillments, each on its own day. He died at Passover, lay in the grave through Unleavened Bread, rose on Firstfruits, and poured out the Spirit on Shavuot. Then comes silence: a four-month stretch of ordinary time between Shavuot and the seventh month. The spring feasts were fulfilled at His first coming. The fall feasts are still waiting. We are living in that gap.

Trumpets — One Sequence in Three Moments

Lev 23:23-25 | Num 10 | Joel 2 | Neh 8:9-10 | Isa 26:20-21 | Matt 24:31, 36 | 1 Thess 4:16 | 1 Cor 15:52

Yom Teruah, the Day of the Shout, is the only feast in the calendar with no past event attached to it. Passover looks back to Egypt. Firstfruits looks back to a harvest. Trumpets is given with nothing behind it: a Sabbath, a memorial, a blowing of trumpets (Leviticus 23:23-25). It faces forward from the moment it is given. It was also the one feast whose date could not be fixed in advance. It falls on the first day of the seventh month, and a Hebrew month began only when witnesses confirmed the new moon. Yeshua places the exact vocabulary of this feast on His own return: "He will send out His angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather His elect" (Matthew 24:31). Then, in the same breath: "Of that day and hour no one knows" (24:36). Paul hears the same sound in two different letters (1 Thessalonians 4:16, 1 Corinthians 15:52).

When I studied this feast (2026-09-10), Ryan pressed on a tension that looked like a contradiction. Joel calls for fasting, weeping, and mourning at the trumpet. Nehemiah 8 tells the people on this very day, "do not mourn… the joy of Yahweh is your strength." The tension resolved once I saw them as three moments in one sequence, not one moment described two ways:

Moment 1 — The Alarm

Teruah is the broken, wailing blast, the sound of alarm (Numbers 10). Joel 2 is that alarm at full scale: "Blow the trumpet in Zion… return to Me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning." This is the response when what the feast rehearses is visibly, urgently arriving. It is corporate. No one is exempt, not even the bride and groom (Joel 2:16).

Moment 2 — The Rehearsal, and Its Joy

Nehemiah 8 is the ordinary yearly keeping of the feast, after repentance has already happened. The people weep as the Torah is read, and they are told to stop and to eat, drink, and send portions to those who have nothing. Joy is the fruit of having turned. It does not replace turning. Every year the trumpet rehearses an alarm that has not yet sounded for real.

Moment 3 — The Day Itself

Isaiah 26:20-21 names what the righteous do when the day of wrath actually arrives: "Come, my people, enter your chambers, and shut your doors behind you; hide yourselves for a little while until the fury has passed by." This is not feasting and not fighting. It is taking refuge, in a place Yahweh appoints.

What Held: My underlying position held steady under real pressure: the fall feasts point forward, Trumpets is tied to a coming gathering, and the right posture is watchfulness without setting dates. What I gained was the sequence itself: alarm, rehearsal-joy, and the actual day. I am recording that as working ground, so a future session starts here and does not re-derive it from scratch.

The Shelter Thread — One Architecture Across the Whole Canon

Gen 7:16 | Ex 12:13, 23 | Josh 2:18-21 | Isa 26:20-21 | John 14:2-3

Isaiah's hidden chambers opened something I had not named before as one continuous thread. I had seen each of these separately. I had never seen them as the same design:

  • Noah: "and Yahweh shut him in" (Genesis 7:16). Yahweh closed the door Himself, before the flood came.
  • Passover: Israel stayed behind the blood on the doorposts while the destroyer passed over (Exodus 12:13, 23).
  • Rahab: the scarlet cord in the window, and everyone inside the house kept while the walls fell (Joshua 2:18-21).
  • Isaiah: enter your chambers and shut the doors until the fury has passed (Isaiah 26:20-21).

Each time, Yahweh appoints the shelter, and His people enter it before His judgment lands. They are sealed in and wait it out. They are never told to fight His wrath. They are told to take refuge from it in the place He provides. And the night before He died, Yeshua described the last shelter in the same terms: "In My Father's house are many rooms… I go to prepare a place for you" (John 14:2-3).

This thread now carries weight in two other places in this document. In the Pre/Mid/Post tab, it sharpens the honest question "shelter before what?" In the world religion scores, it is the reason Biblical Faith's answer to evil and suffering now scores 10.

Yom Kippur — Near and Far

Lev 16 | Ex 40:17 | Heb 9-10 | Zech 12:10 | Rom 11:26-27 | Matt 25:31-46 | Rev 20:7-15 | b. Yoma 39b

Leviticus 16 was given in a narrow window. The tabernacle was raised on the first day of the first month of the second year (Exodus 40:17). Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu died soon after, and Leviticus 16 opens "after the death of the two sons of Aaron." The Day of Atonement, and the whole feast calendar of Leviticus 23 with it, was legislated in that same short stretch. On that day, the High Priest went behind the veil once, in plain linen. A bull was offered for his own house. One goat was offered for Yahweh. The other goat, the scapegoat, had the nation's sins confessed over its head and was led away, never to return. Hebrews 9 says what it pictured: Messiah entered "once for all," not with the blood of goats but His own, and the veil Aaron could pass only in fear was torn from top to bottom.

One record belongs here because it comes from Israel's own tradition, not from a Christian source. The Babylonian Talmud (Yoma 39b) records that for "the forty years prior to the destruction of the Second Temple, the lot did not arise in the High Priest's right hand at all," the crimson strip tied to the scapegoat "did not turn white," the westernmost lamp "did not burn continually," and "the doors of the Sanctuary opened by themselves." Forty years before 70 AD lands around 30 AD, the window of the crucifixion (Luke 3:1 sets John's ministry in the fifteenth year of Tiberius). The Talmud records the omens and does not draw that connection; the dates do.

A second, independent rabbinic tradition lands in the same forty-years window. The Jerusalem Talmud (Sanhedrin 1:1, 18a, and Sanhedrin 7:2, 24b) and the Babylonian Talmud (Shabbat 15a; Avodah Zarah 8b) separately record that the Sanhedrin's authority to try capital cases was removed roughly forty years before the Temple fell — the same c. 30 AD window as the omens above, reached by a completely different line of rabbinic tradition. This is also why John 18:31 has the Jewish leaders tell Pilate "it is not lawful for us to put anyone to death" — a real historical constraint, not a narrative convenience. (I checked this against a quote widely circulated in Christian apologetics — "Woe to us, the scepter has departed from Judah and Shiloh has not come" — commonly attributed to Sanhedrin 37a. Checked directly against that page, the quote is not there; it appears to trace to an uncorroborated apologetics source rather than the Talmud itself, and I am not using it. The four tractates above are the real, checkable record, and they are enough on their own.)

The Ten Days Between Trumpets and Yom Kippur

The question is where the ten days between Trumpets and Yom Kippur (the Days of Awe) land in the future timeline. They do not land at the final rebellion after the thousand years: people who lived under Messiah's visible reign for a thousand years and still rebel are not given a fresh window to turn, and Revelation 20:7-9 shows fire falling at once. They are also more than a picture of each person's own urgency, because Yahweh was concrete and precise with every spring feast and the fall feasts are no less exact. The ten days belong to the turning that comes close to the trumpet. (2026-09-20)

What the Word Shows

Every spring feast carries a doubled fulfillment, as the table above shows. Yom Kippur carries the same shape. Near: Israel's national mourning and turning to Messiah, landing close to the trumpet, as the ten days require. "They will look on Me, on Him whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for Him" (Zechariah 12:10). "All Israel will be saved… when I take away their sins" (Romans 11:26-27). Far: the Great White Throne, the final and complete opening of the books, after the thousand years (Revelation 20:11-15). Both are real. Neither cancels the other.

Keeping the Sequence in Order

The order matters, and it is checked again every time it is restated. Yeshua's coronation and the Great White Throne are a thousand years apart. In order: Messiah returns as King, and the living nations are judged (sheep and goats, Matthew 25:31-46). The thousand years follow, with real and costly obedience still required (Zechariah 14:16-19). A final rebellion is ended by fire from heaven (Revelation 20:7-9), not by the bowl judgments. Then comes the Great White Throne (Revelation 20:11-15).

Sukkot — The Feast of the Kingdom

Lev 23:39-43 | Zech 14:16-19 | John 1:14 | Rev 21:3

Sukkot is the last feast and the fullest. Israel lived in temporary shelters, remembering the wilderness, and rejoiced over the completed harvest (Leviticus 23:39-43). Its destination is named outright. Zechariah 14:16-19 shows every nation going up to Jerusalem year after year to keep this feast under the King's own rule, which is the argument the Millennial Proof tab builds on. Its last word is Yahweh dwelling with His people: "the dwelling place of God is with man" (Revelation 21:3). John uses the feast's own image for the incarnation: the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us (John 1:14, eskēnōsen).

What This Rests On

The spring feasts are history, checked and fulfilled. The fall feasts are prediction, and the spring feasts set their shape:

  • Near and far for Yom Kippur follows the pattern kept in every spring feast. Zechariah 12:10 and Romans 11:26-27 name the near, and Revelation 20:11-15 the far.
  • No date-setting. "Of that day and hour no one knows" (Matthew 24:36). The hour was withheld. The season was not: learn from the fig tree (24:32-33). Watchfulness, not calculation.
  • The sequence above is the order the text gives: Matthew 25:31-46, Zechariah 14:16-19, then Revelation 20:7-15.
  • Resonance is not exegesis. Revelation 6:6's "do not harm the oil and the wine" invites a modern petroleum reading. The Greek word is literal olive oil, and nothing in the language supports that reading. When a listener notices a connection like that, it is resonance. It is not what the verse says. In the same way, Isaiah 66:8 ("shall a nation be born in one day?") alongside Israel's rebirth on May 14, 1948 is a real convergence worth noticing. It is not proof of a timeline.
  • Every generation has read 2 Timothy 3 as a mirror of its own day. That does not make the discernment wrong. It means real watchfulness has to be sturdy enough to hold "this might be it" without collapsing if it is not yet.
Finding: Yahweh's calendar is not a relic of Israel's past. It is a schedule He has already kept, four times, to the day. That is part of why I hold, at 97%, that what He called "a statute forever" was never cancelled. The fall feasts tell us what comes next: a trumpet, a nation turning, a final reckoning, and Yahweh dwelling with His people. The question they have always asked is not "when?" It is "will you be found inside the shelter when it comes?" His name is Yeshua: Yah saves. (Written 2026-10-03.)
