The Critical Question: What Happens to Sinai?

Every other covenant is universally agreed to be eternal — no one disputes that the Noahic rainbow covenant still stands, or that God's promises to Abraham are still in effect, or that the Davidic throne promise applies to Messiah. The entire debate centers on one covenant: Sinai (Mosaic). Is it abolished? Replaced? Renewed? Continued? The answer to this single question determines which denomination "gets it right."

Traditional Western Position

The Mosaic Covenant was a temporary administration for Israel until Messiah came. Christ fulfilled it, and the New Covenant replaces it. Christians are under grace, not law. The "moral law" (Ten Commandments minus Sabbath) continues, but ceremonial and civil law are abolished. Key texts: Galatians 3:19-25 ("the law was our schoolmaster"), Hebrews 8:13 ("obsolete"), 2 Corinthians 3:7-11 ("ministry of death... passing away"), Colossians 2:14 ("nailing it to the cross").

Torah-Continuity Position

The Mosaic Covenant was never described by God as temporary. Psalm 111:7-8 says His commandments "stand fast forever and ever." The "New Covenant" of Jeremiah 31:31-33 does not introduce new laws — it writes the same Torah on hearts instead of stone. Yeshua explicitly said He did not come to abolish Torah (Matt 5:17-19). The perceived "abolition" texts are about the penalty of the law (the death sentence for sin) being nailed to the cross — not the instructions themselves. The schoolmaster (Gal 3:24) led us to Christ — it didn't self-destruct upon arrival.

The Abrahamic Covenant — Foundation of Everything

Gen 12:1-3 | Gen 15:1-21 | Gen 17:1-14 | Gen 22:15-18 | Gen 26:4-5 | Gal 3:8, 16-18, 29

God made an unconditional, eternal covenant with Abraham — land, seed, and blessing to all nations. This is the foundation that every subsequent covenant builds upon, never replaces. Critically, Genesis 26:4-5 reveals why Abraham received these promises:

עֵקֶב אֲשֶׁר־שָׁמַע אַבְרָהָם בְּקֹלִי וַיִּשְׁמֹר מִשְׁמַרְתִּי מִצְוֺתַי חֻקּוֹתַי וְתוֹרֹתָי
"ʿeqev ʾašer-šāmaʿ ʾavrāhām bĕqōlî wayyišmōr mišmartî miṣwōtay ḥuqqōtay wĕtōrōtāy"
"Because Abraham obeyed My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes, and My Torot (plural of Torah)."

This is before Sinai — roughly 430 years before Moses. Abraham kept God's commandments, statutes, and Torot. The same Hebrew words (מִצְוֺת ,חֻקּוֹת ,תּוֹרֹת) used later at Sinai. This means Torah-principles predate Sinai. They were not invented at Sinai; they were codified there.

Finding: Torah instruction predates the Mosaic Covenant. Sinai organized what already existed. This undermines the claim that Torah was a temporary Sinai-only arrangement.

The Mosaic / Sinai Covenant — What God Actually Said

Ex 19:5-6 | Ex 24:7-8 | Ex 31:16-17 | Deut 4:2 | Deut 29:14-15 | Ps 111:7-8 | Ps 119:160

At Sinai, God made a conditional covenant with Israel: "If you obey My voice and keep My covenant, you shall be My own possession among all peoples" (Ex 19:5). The Torah was given as the terms. But note four critical features that most traditions overlook:

1. God never called it temporary. Psalm 111:7-8 — "All His precepts are trustworthy; they are established forever and ever (לָעַד וּלְעוֹלָם, lāʿad ûlĕʿôlām — a double-eternal construction)." Psalm 119:160 — "The sum of Your word is truth, and every one of Your righteous ordinances is everlasting (עוֹלָם)."

2. It was not only for ethnic Israel. Deuteronomy 29:14-15 — "Not with you alone do I make this covenant and this oath, but with him who stands here with us today before the LORD our God, and with him who is not here with us today." This explicitly extends the covenant beyond those physically present — to future generations and to all who join themselves to Yahweh (Isaiah 56:6-8).

3. The Sabbath is called an eternal sign. Exodus 31:16-17 — "The sons of Israel shall observe the Sabbath... as a perpetual covenant (בְּרִית עוֹלָם, bĕrît ʿôlām). It is a sign between Me and the sons of Israel forever." (What the Sabbath command actually asks is examined word by word in the card below.)

4. The covenant opens with love, not with a rulebook. When I studied the First Commandment against its full set of cross-references (2026-07-11), the survey did not just add supporting verses. It changed how I hold the commandment in four places:

  • Its rank. It is not the first of ten equals. Yeshua names it the root that "all the Torah and the Prophets hang on" (Matthew 22:37-40). The other nine hang from it.
  • Its shape. The Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4-5) shows the prohibition "no other gods before Me" is the negative outline of a positive command: love Yahweh with all your heart, soul, and strength. It should be introduced as a love command first and a prohibition second.
  • Its scope. When John falls at the feet of an angel, a genuine messenger of God, he is told, "Do not do that… worship God" (Revelation 22:8-9). So the line this commandment draws is Creator versus creature, not real versus fake or evil versus good. Something can be true, holy, and sent by God, and still not be God.
  • How it breaks. Scripture shows at least three distinct ways it fails, not one: outright replacement of Yahweh with something else, divided loyalty that keeps Him alongside a rival, and slow relational drift away from Him.

This matters for the whole covenant argument. If the covenant's first word is love, then the covenant was never the cold legal code the "law versus grace" framing assumes. It was a relationship with terms from its very first line, which is exactly what Jeremiah 31:33 says will be written on the heart.

Finding: The Hebrew text describes Torah's ordinances as עוֹלָם (olam — perpetual/eternal) and the Sabbath as בְּרִית עוֹלָם (an eternal covenant). If "eternal" means temporary, then every promise God makes — including salvation — is unreliable.

The Sabbath Command, Word by Word — What the Fourth Commandment Actually Asks

Ex 20:8-11 | Num 15:32-36 | Luke 4:16 | Mark 2:23-28 | John 5 | Acts 15:21 | Col 2:16-17

I will be honest about where I started. When I studied this commandment on its own (2026-07-11), my opening position was hedged and untested: a weekly rest tied to creation, which much of the Western church treats as fulfilled in Christ or moved to Sunday, and I had never actually checked that assumption against the text. This card is what the text did to that position.

zākhôr (זָכוֹר) — "Remember." It does not mean passive recollection, as in "keep it in mind." It is an active, ongoing verb: remembering by doing. The command is to keep acting on the day, week after week, not to recall that it exists.

leqaddeshô (לְקַדְּשׁוֹ) — "to keep it holy." The person does not make the day holy. Yahweh already sanctified it at creation (Genesis 2:3). The command is to actively enact a holiness that already exists, not to originate one.

melākhāh (מְלָאכָה) — "work." I first framed this as occupational labor, your trade or your job. Ryan corrected me with the wood-gatherer of Numbers 15:32-36, a man gathering sticks, not working his trade. The standard is any act of production, however small. That correction was his, it was right, and I hold it now.

Shabbāt la-YHWH — "a Sabbath to Yahweh." The preposition gives the day a direction. The rest is offered to Him. It is not neutral personal time off.
The Hardest Texts, Given Their Full Weight

Mark 2:27 — "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath." This is the verse most often used to loosen the command, so I tested it rather than assuming it either way. "Made for man" says the Sabbath is a gift given for people's good. It does not say people own it or may set it aside. And in the very next verse Yeshua claims to be Lord of the Sabbath (Mark 2:28). You claim lordship over something that still stands, not something you are about to abolish.

Colossians 2:16-17 — read in context, it argues that the Sabbath still casts a real shadow of "things to come." A shadow needs a substance standing behind it. That is a picture of something continuing, not something cancelled. (The full Greek is in the 12 Key Arguments tab, passage 5.)

What the New Testament Actually Shows

Yeshua kept the Sabbath as His lifelong custom (Luke 4:16). His Sabbath controversies in Mark 2 and John 5 are about how to keep it, never whether to. Paul kept going to the synagogue on the Sabbath for decades in Acts. The Jerusalem Council simply assumes Gentile believers will be hearing Moses read "every Sabbath" (Acts 15:21). I went in expecting the New Testament data to lean toward the command being relaxed. Examined text by text, it consistently does not.

Finding: My position on this commandment actually reversed, and I am recording it as a reversal, not as a deepening. The Fourth Commandment asks for an active, ongoing, God-directed rest from any act of production, honoring a holiness Yahweh already placed on the day. Nothing in the New Testament texts I examined cancels it. (Studied 2026-07-11, added here 2026-10-03.)

The New Covenant — Replacement or Renewal?

Jer 31:31-34 | Ezek 36:26-27 | Heb 8:8-12 | Luke 22:20 | 2 Cor 3:3-6

This is the most consequential passage in the entire debate. Jeremiah 31:31-33:

הִנֵּה יָמִים בָּאִים נְאֻם־יְהוָה וְכָרַתִּי אֶת־בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאֶת־בֵּית יְהוּדָה בְּרִית חֲדָשָׁה
"Behold, days are coming, declares YHWH, when I will cut with the house of Israel and the house of Judah a bĕrît ḥădāšāh — a new covenant"

כִּי זֹאת הַבְּרִית אֲשֶׁר אֶכְרֹת אֶת־בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל... נָתַתִּי אֶת־תּוֹרָתִי בְּקִרְבָּם וְעַל־לִבָּם אֶכְתְּבֶנָּה
"For this is the covenant I will cut with the house of Israel... I will put My Torah within them and write it on their hearts"

The critical word is חֲדָשָׁה (ḥădāšāh) — "new." In Hebrew, this word means renewed, refreshed, restored — like the "new moon" (חֹדֶשׁ, ḥōdeš) which is the same moon made new, not a different moon. Compare this to the Greek καινή (kainē) used in Hebrews 8:8 — which can mean either "brand new" or "renewed in quality." The Septuagint translators chose kainē for ḥădāšāh, preserving the ambiguity.

Traditional Reading

The New Covenant replaces the Old. Hebrews 8:13 says the first covenant is "obsolete" and "ready to vanish away." The law was a temporary guardian. The New Covenant is fundamentally different in kind.

Torah-Continuity Reading

Read Jeremiah 31:33 carefully: God does not say "I will write new laws on their hearts." He says "I will write My Torah (תּוֹרָתִי — tôrātî)" — the same Torah, same possessive "My." The change is the location (stone → heart) and the mechanism (external compliance → internal desire through the Spirit), not the content. Ezekiel 36:27 confirms: "I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes (חֻקּוֹתַי, the same word from Sinai)."

What the Text Actually Says

Jeremiah 31:33 is explicit: the content of the New Covenant is Torah — written internally rather than externally. Ezekiel 36:27 confirms the Spirit's role is to cause Torah-obedience, not eliminate its need. Hebrews 8:13 says the first covenant (the Sinai administration — the stone tablets, the Levitical priesthood mediation system) is made obsolete — not the Torah content itself. The author of Hebrews immediately quotes Jeremiah 31, which explicitly preserves Torah. To read Hebrews 8:13 as "Torah is abolished" would contradict the very passage the author is quoting as his proof text.

Finding: Jeremiah 31:33 explicitly identifies the content of the New Covenant as "My Torah" written on hearts. The New Covenant is a renewed administration of the same Torah — internalized by the Spirit — not a replacement with different content.

Who Is the Gentile Believer? — The Grafting In, Then and Now

This is one of the most misunderstood questions in scripture — and one of the most beautiful when it becomes clear. If you have ever wondered whether you belong, whether the covenants of Israel include you, whether you are on the outside looking in — this is what the evidence says. Start here. Let the pieces fall into place.

Then — The Door Was Always Open

Long before Yeshua, Yahweh made clear that belonging to His covenant family was never about ethnicity. It was always about a choice — to turn, to join, to say: Your God shall be my God.

Ruth 1:16 — The Model

"Where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God."

Ruth was a Moabite — an outsider by every ethnic measure. She made one declaration of loyalty. She was received as native-born. She appears in the genealogy of Yeshua (Matthew 1:5). The door was not opened for the first time at Pentecost. It was always open to anyone who came through faith.

Numbers 15:16 — One Law, One Household

"There shall be one statute, one rule, for you and for the stranger who sojourns with you."

Yahweh declared this at Sinai — before the New Covenant was even prophesied. The standard was never ethnic. A foreigner who joined the covenant community lived under the same Torah. Not a watered-down version. The same. One household. One instruction. One God.

Isaiah 56:6-7 — The Explicit Invitation

"Foreigners who join themselves to Yahweh, to minister to him, to love the name of Yahweh, and to be his servants — everyone who keeps the Sabbath and does not profane it, and holds fast my covenant — these I will bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer... for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples."

Yeshua quoted this passage when He drove out the money changers. The Temple was always meant to be for all peoples — and the way in was always the same: love the Name, keep the Sabbath, hold fast the covenant.

Now — What Changes When You Come to Faith in Yeshua

Here is what Paul says happened to you — as a Gentile — the moment you came to faith. Read it carefully. This is not poetry. This is a legal covenant declaration about your status.

Ephesians 2:12-13, 19 — Before and After

Before: "...you were at that time separated from Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world."

After: "But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ... So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God."

You were outside the covenants of promise. All of them — Abrahamic, Mosaic, Davidic, New. You had no claim on any of them. Through Yeshua, you were brought in. Not to a separate institution. Into the same household — Israel's household — as a full member.

Romans 11:17-18 — The Olive Tree Picture

"But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, although a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among the others and now share in the nourishing root of the olive tree, do not be arrogant toward the branches. If you are, remember it is not you who support the root, but the root that supports you."

One tree. Not two. Israel's tree — rooted in the Abrahamic covenant. Some Jewish branches were broken off for unbelief. Wild branches — Gentiles — were grafted in. The tree remains Israel's tree. The root remains the covenant with Abraham. The Gentile believer draws life from that root. Not a new tree. Not a parallel grove. The same tree.

Galatians 3:29 — You Are Abraham's Offspring

"And if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise."

This is the clearest statement in the New Testament. In Messiah, a Gentile becomes Abraham's seed — not metaphorically, but covenantally. The promises made to Abraham now apply to you: land, blessing, a people, a God who keeps His word across generations.

The Journey — What It Looks Like in Practice

You don't have to figure out how to be Jewish. You have to figure out how to be faithful — to the same God, through the same Messiah, in the same household. Here is what that looks like:

You were outside

Strangers to the covenants of promise. No claim on the Sabbath, the feasts, the household. Outside the gate.

Yeshua opens the door

By His blood, you are brought near. Not as a guest. As a family member. "Fellow citizens with the saints." Full household status.

The Spirit equips you

Ezekiel 36:27 — "I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes." The Torah is now written on your heart. The Spirit is the one who moves you toward obedience — not rules, relationship.

Same household, same Torah

Numbers 15:16 still stands. One law for native-born and sojourner. The Sabbath is yours now (Isaiah 56:6). The feasts are yours (Zechariah 14:16 — all nations). The covenant meal is yours. The same life that flows through the root flows through your branch.

Honor the root

Romans 11:18 — "It is not you who supports the root, but the root that supports you." The Gentile believer does not stand above Israel. The covenant family existed long before you arrived. Enter with gratitude, not arrogance.

One new man — together

Ephesians 2:15 — "one new man in place of the two." Not Gentile separate from Jew. Not Jew superior to Gentile. One. Together. The wall broken down. The family complete in Messiah.

Where This Road Ends — Zechariah 8:23

"Thus says Yahweh of hosts: In those days ten men from the nations of every tongue shall take hold of the robe of a Jew, saying, 'Let us go with you, for we have heard that God is with you.'"

The nations do not replace Israel. They come to Israel. They say: take us with you. They grab the robe and walk together into the presence of God. That is the prophetic completion of the New Covenant — not a Gentile church standing where Israel stood, but Gentile and Jew walking together toward the One who grafted them into the same tree.

That is simple. That is beautiful. That is Yeshua.

Finding: The Gentile believer is not outside the covenants. Not a second-class member. Not grafted around Israel — grafted in. By grace through faith in Yeshua, they enter the same household, live under the same Torah written by the same Spirit, honor the same Sabbath, and walk toward the same God. The family the Father always intended — every nation, every tongue, one tree, one root, one Name.
