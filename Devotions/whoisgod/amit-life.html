99.97% Yeshua is who the Hebrew prophets said He would be. ▶

The evidence: The Hebrew prophets described the Messiah with mathematical precision across 39 books written over 1,500 years: birthplace (Micah 5:2 — Bethlehem), timing (Daniel 9:24-26 — 483 years from the decree of Artaxerxes to the exact week of the triumphal entry), manner of death (Psalm 22 — crucifixion described a thousand years before Rome invented it), resurrection (Psalm 16:10 — "you will not let your Holy One see decay"), and the divine nature (Isaiah 9:6 — "Mighty God, Everlasting Father").

The manuscripts predate the event they're accused of being shaped by: The Great Isaiah Scroll, found at Qumran and dated to roughly 125 BC — over a century before Yeshua — contains the full text of Isaiah 53 essentially as it reads today, closing off any claim that the chapter was edited after the fact to fit a crucified Messiah. One honest variant exists at 53:9 (the scroll reads "his tomb," the Masoretic Text "his deaths") — worth naming so the match isn't overstated. Psalm 22:16 differs among the witnesses: the Masoretic Text reads "like a lion," which leaves the clause without a verb, while the Greek translation made before Yeshua (LXX Psalm 21:17, "they dug") and the Dead Sea Scrolls fragment from Nahal Hever (dated decades before the Temple fell, a Jewish document with no Christian hand in it) read a verb: "they pierced." Separately, physical archaeology confirms crucified men could receive family burial: the 1968 discovery at Giv'at ha-Mivtar of Yehohanan, a crucified man buried with the nail still through his heel bone, in a family tomb — the only direct physical evidence of Roman crucifixion ever excavated, and real grounds against the claim that a crucified man's body would simply have been left unburied.

The resurrection: The best historical explanation of all known facts by the standard historians apply to any ancient event. The tomb was empty — attested by enemies who tried to explain it, not deny it. The disciples went from hiding to publicly proclaiming the resurrection within weeks, willing to die for this testimony. Over 500 people claimed to have seen Him alive (1 Corinthians 15:6), including, by Paul's own early account (1 Corinthians 15:7), Yeshua's own brother James — who during His life did not believe Him (John 7:5), yet became a leader of the Jerusalem church (Galatians 1:19, 2:9) and was stoned to death for that testimony around 62 AD, recorded not by a Christian source but by Josephus (Antiquities 20.9.1). Movements built around an executed messianic claimant in this period did not normally survive the claimant's death — Bar Kokhba's did not, and neither did the earlier movements of Theudas or Judas the Galilean (Acts 5:36-37). This one grew, in the city of the execution, on the specific claim of a bodily resurrection. The movement could have been stopped at any point by producing a body. It was never produced.

One more hostile source, honestly caveated: The Babylonian Talmud (Sanhedrin 43a) records that "on the eve of Passover Yeshu was hanged," after a herald announced for forty days that he was charged with sorcery and leading Israel astray — a hostile source confirming the Passover timing and the charge of being a dangerous wonder-worker. The Talmud elsewhere (Sanhedrin 107b) places a Yeshu two centuries earlier, in the Hasmonean period, so this passage stands as a hostile witness to the Passover timing and the charge of sorcery. The case itself rests on the Gospels, Tacitus and Josephus.

What challenged this: The strongest objection is the late dating of some Gospel manuscripts. The evidence: Paul's letters, written within 20 years of the resurrection, contain the full resurrection claim (1 Corinthians 15:3-8) and reference the Jerusalem community where the tomb was a walk away.
