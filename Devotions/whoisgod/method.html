Purpose: Christians have debated the relationship between Old and New Covenants for two millennia. This analysis does not begin with a conclusion — it begins with the texts themselves, in their oldest verifiable forms, and follows the evidence wherever it leads.

Method: (1) Examine each covenant on its own terms — what did God actually say? (2) Trace the Hebrew and Greek words that are commonly mistranslated or misapplied. (3) Present both the traditional Western Christian position and the Torah-continuity position with full scriptural support. (4) Apply the "Millennial test" — if a practice exists in the Messianic Kingdom under Yeshua's direct rule, it cannot logically have been abolished. (5) Let the evidence speak for itself — without tradition, without denomination, without agenda.

Manuscript Priority: Where translations diverge, this analysis prioritizes the Masoretic Text (Hebrew OT), Dead Sea Scrolls (oldest OT manuscripts, ~250 BC–70 AD), Septuagint (LXX, Greek OT used by apostles), and the earliest Greek NT manuscripts (Codex Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, papyri). Aramaic Targumim and Peshitta provide interpretive context.

Fairness Commitment: Every tradition represented here is held sincerely by millions of believers. This analysis respects that. The goal is not to attack any tradition — it is to let the evidence speak for itself, even when that challenges deeply held assumptions on any side.

A Note to Every Reader

Wherever you are in your walk — whatever tradition you come from — this analysis takes your position seriously and presents its strongest scriptural arguments before examining counter-evidence. Whether you hold that the Torah is fulfilled and no longer binding, or whether you observe Sabbath and feasts — both positions are treated with the respect they deserve. No tradition is mocked. Every tradition is measured by the same standard: the text itself, in its original languages, read honestly. The goal is not to win an argument. It is to walk alongside every sincere reader toward greater light.
